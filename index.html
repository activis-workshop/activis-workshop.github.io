Overview

Vision foundation models are trained on passive data, encoding the world as pixels or linguistic tokens. Robots, in contrast, must reason about physically-grounded quantities like 6D pose, dynamics, and affordances. This workshop forges a bidirectional co-design channel to translate perceptual abstractions into actionable structures for control and planning. We will explore two key directions: (vision-to-robotics) defining novel model outputs linked to robot-centric visual semantics, and (robotics-to-vision) learning control-ready representations that directly interface with planning and motor controllers. Our goal is to close the loop from pixels to torque, enabling a new generation of task-hardened, physically-aware AI for robotics.

Topics

This workshop is designed to forge a crucial bridge between the robotics and computer vision communities, moving beyond mere model deployment to pioneer new paradigms of task-driven, co-designed perception-action loops. Our central focus is on discussing what are the critical challenges of translating rich visual signals into precise, safe, and actionable control commands that enable robots to interact intelligently and reliably with the physical world. To ground this mission, we will explore cutting-edge applications across three key interactive dimensions:

  • What visual capability is most urgently needed for fully autonomous systems
  • What can the vision community contribute towards developing general-purpose robotic systems
  • What data modality is most helpful in pretraining for generalizable, robust robot control

Moving from what to how, we will dissect the entire technical pipeline to address concrete challenges at every stage:

  • Data: What is the gap in data curation & synthesis for robots and vision? How to move beyond curated static data to collecting data rich in physical interactions, failures, and multi-modal sensors, with documentation on edge cases to teach models about risks.
  • Model: Does robotics need physics-informed and physics-grounded vision models? Designing networks that inherently reason about physical properties, 3D geometry, and differentiable cause-effect relationships, as well as potential actions.
  • Optimization: Developing methods that embed safety constraints, task-level rewards, and uncertainty quantification directly into the learning objective.
  • Evaluation: What type of system-level evaluation can be established from the vision community? Establishing new metrics and benchmarks that measure real-world task success, robustness to edge cases, and overall system reliability—not just offline score.

By connecting high-level application goals with low-level implementation details, this workshop will catalyze the design of actionable visual perception, and consequently the development of robust, reliable, and real-world-ready robotic systems. The ultimate objective is to foster a deep, two-way dialogue: enabling vision researchers to incorporate robotic context and constraints directly into their model design and training, while empowering roboticists to better understand, trust, and effectively deploy these advanced vision models for diagnosis, adaptation, and successful real-world integration.

Invited Speakers

Jitendra Malik

University of California, Berkeley

Jitendra Malik is the Arthur J. Chick Professor of Electrical Engineering and Computer Sciences at the University of California, Berkeley, and serves as VP of Robotics Research at Meta AI Research (FAIR). Prof. Malik is a preeminent leader in computer vision and machine learning. His research focuses on enabling machines to see and understand the visual world, spanning topics from image segmentation and object recognition to computational modeling of human vision and robotics.

Chelsea Finn

Stanford University

Chelsea Finn is an Assistant Professor in Computer Science and Electrical Engineering at Stanford University and co-founder of Pi. Prof. Finn is a leader in robotics and machine learning, with a focus on meta-learning, reinforcement learning, and robot learning. Her work involves enabling robots to adapt to new tasks with minimal supervision, emphasizing generalization and learning from experience. Specifically, she has worked on vision-based learning and robotic control, with groundbreaking contributions to learning from demonstrations, self-supervised learning, and sim-to-real transfer in robotics.

Nathan F. Lepora

University of Bristol

Nathan F. Lepora is a Professor of Robotics and AI at the University of Bristol. His research focuses on embodied cognition, robot touch, and haptic feedback in robotic systems. His work explores how robots can combine visual and tactile sensing to enhance their interactions with the environment and improve actionable decision-making.

Florian T. Pokorny

KTH Royal Institute of Technology

Florian T. Pokorny is an Associate Professor of Machine Learning in KTH Royal Institute of Technology. His research focuses on data-driven robotic manipulation, including training data requirements, transfer learning, and cloud robotics. His work integrates deep learning, geometric, and topological methods to enhance robot-object interaction, particularly in deformable and fragile object manipulation.
